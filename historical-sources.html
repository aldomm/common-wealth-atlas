Historical boundary coverage and audit

Worldwide reconstruction from 1820: Cliopatria

The unified timeline automatically uses CShapes for 1886–2019 and Cliopatria for other supported years. This provider switch is not itself a historical event; their coding differs. Source alternatives remain in a collapsed control. Outside all historical coverage, dated history remains unavailable while present-day reference areas remain visible and selectable. Statistics keep their own source years; nearby loaded MPD snapshots are separately labeled reference evidence, never backdated. The earlier-period view uses Seshat Cliopatria v0.2.1, pinned commit 5f433377139a6eeaeacbdc894b70f8805b72c8b5. Retrieved 13 November 2026. This is a worldwide historical reconstruction with inclusive annual FromYear/ToYear intervals, not exact-day sovereignty. The atlas retains all 2,869 POLITY records intersecting 1820–2024, representing 521 distinct source names; 153 records are active in 1820. All 205 annual slices have unique active name identifiers. Absence is a coverage gap, not evidence that no polity existed.

All imported source geometries passed GEOS validity checks. Display geometry is simplified with topology preservation at 0.015 degrees and rounded into SVG coordinates; originals and hashes remain retained. Small boundary details may not be visible. Source RELATION features (385 across the complete source archive) are omitted, rather than painted as additional sovereign territories. Source Components/MemberOf identifiers are retained where supplied; their full hierarchical interpretation is not implemented. The audit finds material polygon intersections in all 205 annual slices, including overlapping empire aggregates and component polities. Both are retained; smaller shapes draw above larger ones, with all records available in the selector. These are not a mutually exclusive world partition. Overlapping source claims are not silently dissolved into one owner; the spatial-intersection audit is saved in research/cliopatria-spatial-overlaps.json. A polygon intersection is not automatically an error or a competing sovereign claim.

The source's history is sampled at uneven temporal and spatial scales. Polity names, borders, dates and uncertainties remain scholarly reconstructions open to revision; world coverage does not mean every historical political community or change is captured. It must not be mixed invisibly with CShapes' different coding. The app does not interpolate geometry, extrapolate after 2024 or assign modern economic totals to these shapes.

Dates and identifiers

The global GeoJSON timestamp strings are timezone shifted relative to the explicit date components and the source CSV. All 710 start strings differ by a day. The importer uses gwsyear/gwsmonth/gwsday and the corresponding end components, matching the CSV's date semantics. All 1,392 distinct transition-adjacent dates were checked for duplicate active source IDs; no temporal overlaps were found. Fourteen gaps between records for the same ID are retained and reported, not filled. These can represent absorption into another polity or source omissions; we have not classified all gaps.

Gleditsch–Ward source IDs are preserved and never treated as modern ISO codes. Labels such as Russia (Soviet Union), Germany (Prussia), and Turkey (Ottoman Empire) are longitudinal aliases. They are not official names in every year. No automatic successor substitution or MPD/WDI population/GDP join is performed.

Geometry

All rings were checked for closure, finite coordinates and coordinate range. No antimeridian-spanning ring jumps were found. Eleven source records have invalid self-intersections (Israel, Vietnam and Australia). GEOS make_valid repairs are logged; only polygonal components are rendered. Original downloads are unchanged. SVG uses equirectangular projection and three-decimal coordinate rounding. This is a rendering transformation, not a revision of territorial claims. Adjacent polygons were not globally retopologized and every claim was not manually verified.

Sovereignty and omissions

The global export does not contain the status, ruledby or border-definition fields described in its codebook. The app explicitly says these fields are unavailable; it never infers that every entity was independent. The European file retains its own Status and Holder fields. Colonial/local units remain separate source records where supplied.

The provider's codebook describes exclusions including small territorial changes, some unrecognized secessions and temporary wartime changes. Some dates are defaults and some historically undefined borders were backdated. The 2018 codebook describes coverage through 2017; the current downloadable world file and landing page extend through 2019. The Europe file extends through 2023. Source geometry for Crimea changes in 2014; this is the provider's coding, not an endorsement of sovereignty or a claim that the layer consistently represents internationally recognized borders. Later wars are not extrapolated.

Checks and examples

Automated checks cover every source transition plus its preceding/following day, and all 204 European annual slices from 1820–2023. Representative geometry checks include Romania 1900/1916 and 1913-08-09/10; Russia's 1991-12-20/21 change; India/Pakistan 1947-08-14/15; Sudan/South Sudan 2011-07-08/09. Portugal and Spain retain distinct IDs throughout coverage. Capital-only source changes need not alter polygons. Source chronology itself can differ from other historical accounts.
