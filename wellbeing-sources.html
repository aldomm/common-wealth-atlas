Wellbeing, living standards and historical context

Every map value retains its source and date. These measures answer different questions: self-reported life evaluation, longevity, education, basic services, poverty and income distribution are not interchangeable scores.

How gaps are displayed

  • Source record: a published value at the selected date, including the source's own reconstructed or modeled estimates.
  • Interpolation: a straight-line estimate between compatible stored observations, clearly labeled with both endpoints. GDP and population use gaps no wider than 20 years; eligible continuous indicators use at most 10 years. The formula is A + (B − A) × (selected year − first year)/(last year − first year).
  • Last available: the latest earlier source record stays accessible with its actual date. It is a reference, not an observation or estimate for the selected year.
  • No values are projected before the first source record. No interpolation is applied to happiness survey windows, poverty or Gini survey series, governments, event dates, trade flows or narrative accounts. Published historical AHDI benchmarks remain distinct.

Interpolations smooth unknown volatility. They do not reconstruct the timing or size of shocks, and endpoints from different countries, currencies or source definitions are never combined. Country reference points do not imply that the statistic matches a historical border.

Wars and crises

The initial batch contains 17 institutional-source accounts, with selected country links. One event predates the atlas timeline and remains in the download. Shaded periods on GDP charts provide context; neither overlap nor a before/after difference establishes causation. The event layer uses country reference points, not battle sites or impact areas.
