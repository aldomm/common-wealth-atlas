Population and economy within historical territories

Evidence build, 15 November 2026. 111 verified published numerical values across 14 properties, 37 source years, 19 statistical scopes, and 12 source entries. This is a selective initial batch, not a complete worldwide territorial reconstruction.

What the map now means

Historical territories mode uses source-specific dated boundary records. Six records have reviewed generalized statistical concordances: Great Britain and Ireland, Germany in 1936, unified Germany in 1991, the four-province South African Union, the New Zealand colony in 1896, and Sweden’s resident population for life expectancy in 1906–2009. Other records remain explicitly unmatched. All 3901 loaded boundary records are indexed with geographic fingerprints, dates and provenance.

Reference series & layers preserves all existing country statistics, local evidence, trade, war, basket, wellbeing and comparison tools. These are reference datasets and do not automatically become figures for the historical territory underneath them. Government references remain independently coded.

Evidence rules

  • Official is provenance, not an automatic reliability ranking. Historian reconstructions, other traceable published estimates and app calculations are labeled separately.
  • Source year and geographic compatibility are separate. No modern-country or imperial total silently fills a historical territory gap. Earlier records remain accessible with their dates.
  • Selected-year headlines require compatible geographic scope. A source census date remains a census date, and an annual GDP estimate does not gain exact-day precision.
  • UK census population allows up to ten years between brackets. Sweden life expectancy uses published decade means held constant within each decade; compatible gaps of at most twenty years between period edges can be linearly interpolated. Both require the same reviewed boundary record, date window, source series and definition. Census and mid-year population series are not mixed. No interpolation crosses a split, annexation, merger or source change. No new backcasts are generated.
  • GDP totals require compatible currency, price basis, accounting and year. GDP per person can be computed from aligned historical totals and populations; this is an app calculation. Per-person values are never summed.
  • Regional life-expectancy means from How Was Life? can fill local gaps as explicitly labeled regional proxies. The source’s decade mean is held constant; missing decades may be interpolated between compatible brackets. World proxies are labeled as world estimates. Country/territory-specific compatible evidence takes precedence. These shared averages do not measure local variation and must never be added as totals. Group comparisons retain dated-record requirements; GDP population allocations are offered as a separate, explicitly calculated 1990 PPP series.
  • Component aggregation requires a registered exhaustive disjoint partition including rural coverage. Parent plus child, duplicates, missing components, incompatible units or dates are rejected. No population or GDP is distributed by polygon area.
  • Competing estimates are retained, not silently averaged. Different price benchmarks are not spliced together. Source precision is kept in the registry; headlines are rounded and uncertainty remains unquantified where no defensible interval is published.

Named checks and limitations

UK 1913 uses Great Britain and all Ireland: 45.649 million people and GDP of 224.618 billion 1990 international dollars (Maddison, Tables A1-a/b). Germany 1936 uses 67.336 million people and GDP of 299.753 billion in the same PPP units (Table A-d). The 1936 modern-boundary comparison is kept separate. The German Empire estimate excluding Alsace–Lorraine is not attached to the full 1913 empire.

South Africa 1911 contains the four Union provinces and excludes neighboring protectorates; the component sum reconciles to the published total. New Zealand 1896 combines two published disjoint census groups (743,214), with Māori enumeration in February and general enumeration in April. Its literacy and employment statistics exclude Māori, and literacy also excludes Chinese; they remain subgroup evidence.

Brazil 1872, India 1901, and Japan’s competing national-account estimates have verified values but no completed polygon concordance. They appear as related evidence only. Cliopatria empire/component overlaps, overseas possessions and unresolved frontier discrepancies are not guessed away.

Research still required

Accessible books and papers can contain unofficial estimates worth preserving. Candidate publications remain outside the displayable numeric registry until their values, definitions, pages/tables and geographic support are checked. The publication library adds historical life expectancy and attributed mortality/violence estimates. Narrative accounts are not converted to numerical HDI or happiness scores. Broader coverage, particularly Africa, the Americas and colonial frontiers, remains substantial work.
