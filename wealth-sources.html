Wealth distribution sources

117 observations across 30 countries, with source years 2016–2023. These are household net-wealth shares, not income inequality. Bottom 20%, bottom 40%, top 10% and top 1% overlap and must not be summed.

OECD (2025), Wealth Distribution Database (WDD), key indicators, https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/data/datasets/income-and-wealth-distribution-databases/wdd-key-indicators.xlsx (accessed13November2026).

Source fractions converted to percentages; English sheet used; original values retained. This is an adaptation of an original work by the OECD. The opinions expressed and arguments employed in this adaptation should not be reported as representing the official views of the OECD or of its Member countries.

Original OECD workbook · Definitions · Reuse terms

Great Britain excludes Northern Ireland; Australia uses 2019/20. Country-specific notes and survey periods remain attached to values. Missing observations remain unavailable; no historical extrapolation. Negative bottom shares can occur where debts exceed assets. Employment and social-security pension entitlements are excluded.

Historical WID research is not included in this release: explicit redistribution terms have not been established. The OECD series is not interchangeable with WID equal-split adult estimates.
