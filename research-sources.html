Historical sources & gap filling

We welcome attributed official and unofficial estimates. A source discovery is not a loaded observation. Every value needs a definition, date, place and traceable citation; coverage remains incomplete.

First import

1,677 schooling benchmarks across 138 reference-country series (1820–2010), and 655 infant-mortality decade averages across 126 series (1820s–2000s). The infant averages are held constant within their stated decades, giving 6,550 selectable year-values, not 6,550 independent observations. Two Sudan records remain withheld pending territorial reconciliation.

Schooling covers people aged 15+, distinct from UNDP’s 25+ measure. Infant mortality uses deaths per 1,000 live births and supplies a historical fallback only when no selected-year modern value exists. These source tables use modern 2012 reference geography; they have not been matched to historical borders. Original benchmark values are retained, and schooling interpolation is limited to compatible gaps of at most ten years.

Schooling dataset · CC0 · Infant-mortality dataset · CC BY-SA 4.0. Schooling: Bas van Leeuwen and Jieli van Leeuwen-Li (2015), methodology with Péter Földvári. Infant mortality: Joerg Baten and Mathias Blum (2015). Atlas adaptation: country-reference mapping and repeating each infant-mortality decade average within its source period. The adapted infant-mortality data remain CC BY-SA 4.0. Imported data and citations.

Second import · original-language evidence and larger gaps

17,405 additional source benchmarks: 2,733 population estimates (190 reference-country series), 12,726 life-expectancy records (180), 621 urban-share benchmarks (182), 1,305 birth-cohort height estimates (152), and 20 Portuguese- and Spanish-language census illiteracy records. These are additions to the catalogue, not all newly filled cells.

The coverage audit found 13,331 previously missing, stale-only or regional-only country/property/year cells before 1950 that now have current-year local-reference population or life-expectancy estimates, including bounded interpolation. This is not a count of new independent observations, nor proof of a match to historical borders. Seven Sudan records are withheld because the Atlas’s former-Sudan series has incompatible geography.

Population retains source thousands and converts explicitly to people. Life expectancy retains source benchmark dates; they are not assumed to be decade means. Urban fractions become percentages without time interpolation. Height means adult male-equivalent height for people born in the stated decade, not the height of the population living in each year. Cohort values are held within that decade only. Clio Infra compilation sources can include official figures, reconstructions and conjectures; imported records are conservatively marked published estimates.

Brazilian census illiteracy covers ages 15+. Mexican rates use ages 6+, 10+ or 15+ depending on the census; those are separate measures with no interpolation. Census facts retain their official-compilation label rather than being called Atlas estimates.

Clio datasets, all CC0: Population (Fink-Jensen) · Life expectancy (Zijdeman / Ribeiro da Silva) · Urbanization (Fink-Jensen) · Height (Baten / Blum). Original imported benchmarks and citations. Regional proxies remain labeled regional and are not duplicated as national totals.

Third import · pre-1950 Africa and Asia

3,954 distinct source records across 78 reference countries, including 2,171 before 1950. Coverage includes daily labourer wage/basket ratios, numeracy birth cohorts, historical gross-income Gini, eight African GDP reconstructions, Japanese vital statistics, Cabo Verde birth/death rates, Moroccan population estimates and two explicitly dated Chinese retrospective values. These are additions to the evidence catalogue, not a claim that every record fills a previously empty cell.

GDP reconstructions retain 1990 international dollars, separate from the core 2011 PPP series. Gross-income Gini is separate from modern income/consumption Gini. Daily wages in daily subsistence baskets do not assume annual workdays or family size and are not the annual family-basket ratio. Local occupation coverage is labeled explicitly.

Numeracy is an age-heaping estimate for people born in the source decade, not a contemporary literacy rate. Its value is repeated only within that birth decade. Adaptation: selected African/Asian country references, source-scale checks, and decade-year views. The adapted numeracy dataset remains CC BY-SA 4.0, attributed to Joerg Baten and coauthors; original dataset. Other Clio datasets in this batch are CC0.

Japanese ministry figures retain official-statistics labels and the 1944–1946 gap. Cabo Verde rates are an attributed Portuguese-language historical compilation; inconsistencies in the source’s rates/counts are disclosed. French-language Moroccan former-southern-zone totals are regional estimates, separate from national estimates, with no allocation or invented boundary. Chinese values are explicitly dated 1949 retrospective estimates; undated “old China” statements were not turned into annual data. No new time interpolation is added to this batch.

Exact pages, tables, source rows, original titles, licenses and geographic warnings: imported observations and citations. Current-year annual vital statistics can supplement missing or decade-level core rates; exact existing annual observations remain intact. All series remain accessible through each country’s indicators.

Fourth import · wages and living standards before 1950

3,587 source records and 1,857 separately calculated occupational pay ratios across 40 reference countries. All source periods are before 1950. These are additions to the evidence catalogue, not a claim of complete coverage or nationally representative wages.

The Africa–Asia Occupational Wage Database retains original nominal daily wages, occupations, source currencies, worksheet cells, row notes and country notes. 457 repeated workbook entries were deduplicated; researcher-excluded red cells are withheld. Skilled/labourer pay ratios compare occupations in the same source, year and currency; they are not basic-needs-basket affordability. Source reconstructions and possible source-side interpolation are disclosed. Early Bengal figures are marked as regional proxies under Bangladesh, not Bangladesh national estimates. Unresolved Sudan geography remains withheld.

Japanese-language tables add industrial real-wage indices (1932=100) and men’s and women’s daily pay, preserving survey breaks and printed rounding discrepancies. A French-language Dakar study adds 1938–1944 living-cost and real-minimum-pay indices for four household types. A Dakar index of 68 means 68% of that series’ 1938 purchasing power, not 0.68 baskets. These local indices cannot establish comparable living-standard levels across countries.

Luanda and Benguela decade wages use the April 2024 Carvalhal–Palma working-paper tables, drawing on Portuguese archival sources. They retain city, occupational and historical classifications, and explicitly mark decades the authors interpolated. Decade estimates are repeated only within their stated decade. Nominal pay remains in its original currency, not inflation-adjusted dollars; no new Atlas time interpolation or currency conversion is applied.

Find these measures under each country’s Wealth & income indicators. Click a value for its definition, locality, dates and evidence. Currency-specific wages and independently based local indices cannot be selected for misleading cross-country color comparisons. Imported wage records, source cells and citations. Existing matched basket series remain distinct and were not duplicated.

Fifth import · matched wages and basic-needs baskets

365 new calculated estimates across 11 local or occupational series: 113 Chinese city records (Beijing, Suzhou–Shanghai and Canton), 182 Cape and Natal records, and 70 Japanese servant records. Sources span 1820–1927; coverage remains selective. These supplement the existing nine African matched-basket series without duplicating them.

Family baskets: Chinese and South African estimates use matching source-region/year daily wages and annual adult basket costs. Annual wage assumes 250 paid days; the family budget is three adult equivalents plus 5% housing. Every retained Chinese ratio reconciles with the source workbook’s published ratio. Cape and Natal use wheat barebones baskets and selected cash-pay occupations. Historical racial categories are quoted and contextualized; workers with food or board are not assigned invented cash equivalents.

Adult baskets: Japanese servant estimates use the source-adjusted annual compensation, including modeled board/lodging, divided by 365 days of adult basket costs plus 5% housing. This is a calculated annual-basket measure, distinct from the workbook’s daily-pay ratio using 325 workdays. Advance-payment and calendar assumptions are disclosed. Osaka-based prices and other regional proxies are labeled; they are not worker-by-worker matched prices or national averages. Japanese source titles, including 三井文庫 and 日本帝国統計年鑑, remain in the evidence. The Japanese-language review by Bassino, Ma and Saito (2005), 実質賃金の歴史的水準比較, was checked for context: wage ratios alone omit other household earnings and depend on workdays and basket assumptions; no narrative was converted into numbers.

One basket means one year of the stated adult or family essentials. These ratios are not GDP per person, 2011 international dollars, or an index rebased to 100. Source estimates and imputations remain marked; there is no new Atlas time interpolation. A previous record keeps its actual date. Incompatible locality/occupation series cannot be pooled into a group average. Choose Basic-needs baskets on the map, then a local series; the wage, cost, formula and links to both wage and price evidence are available.

Matched inputs, calculations, source cells and citations. The Angola repository download could not be retrieved in this batch; existing nominal wages are not converted into baskets using guessed costs. Other unmatched regions remain research gaps.

How estimates are handled

  • Prioritize missing country/property/decade combinations, especially pre-1950 and underserved regions.
  • Search national statistics, libraries, census reports, historical gazetteers, university repositories, books and published author estimates in original languages.
  • Keep original titles, translated summaries, exact page/table/row, author, edition, dates, units, geography, evidence class, uncertainty and reuse terms.
  • Retain conflicting claims with provenance; condense only estimates of the same definition, period, territory and unit. A span of estimates is not a confidence interval.
  • Apply a decade or period value only across the period the source actually describes; do not turn an event total into an annual flow.
  • Interpolate compatible numerical series within documented limits; do not interpolate wars, governments, categorical properties or across known definition/territory breaks.
  • Use regional proxies only with explicit geographic scope. Allocate regional totals only with compatible, exhaustive population weights and label the result calculated.
  • No invented numerical precision from narrative-only accounts. Unofficial estimates are eligible when attributable; unsupported claims stay context, not chart values.
  • Audit zeros versus missing codes, country mappings, translations, source licenses, and totals before publishing.

Finding values on the map

Open a country’s indicator list. Values supported for the selected year appear first; dated references and other periods follow. Expand “Available years / periods” to move the shared timeline. The “Hide properties with no data anywhere on the timeline” checkbox is saved on your device. Hiding an empty property does not discard its future research slot.
