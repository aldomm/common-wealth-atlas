Calculated estimates: GDP allocated by population

Country allocation = regional GDP × country population ÷ population of all members. This assumes equal GDP per person across the region. It does not recover actual local productivity, income distribution, wages or wealth.

84 allocations for 12 West European reference countries at seven benchmark years: 1820, 1870, 1913, 1950, 1973, 1990 and 1998. Select “GDP · population allocation” from All indicators, or open the example in Evidence guide. Original country GDP is a separate series and remains unchanged.

Every allocation uses all twelve source populations, including countries outside your selected comparison group. Complete allocations sum to the published regional total. Partial selected groups show only their allocated shares. Missing, duplicate, incompatible or incomplete population inputs block the allocation. Minor rounding differences in the printed population total are disclosed below.

Values are annual output in 1990 PPP international dollars, not nominal or inflation-adjusted US dollars and not the Atlas’s 2011 PPP GDP series. They are calculated reference-territory estimates, not historical-border matches. Before 1950, the UK row includes all Ireland; Germany’s source boundaries also differ by period. Do not add an overlapping territorial or regional total.

Only benchmark years are calculated. A later year may show the last benchmark with its actual date; this is not a new estimate for the selected year. Group graphs require the benchmark year and do not carry it forward.

Inputs: Angus Maddison (2001), The World Economy: A Millennial Perspective. Population: Table A1-a, p.183; GDP: Table A1-b, p.184; geographic definitions: p.177. Allocation is an AMM Tec Lab calculation; Maddison’s published country GDP values may differ substantially.
