Trade across time

The atlas retains separate source datasets. Every available imported annual record remains searchable; the map draws only the largest located connections so it stays readable.

Trade value is gross merchandise value—not government revenue, business profit, wages or household income. Nominal amounts in different years and currencies are not a constant purchasing-power measure.

How to read the network

  • Lines connect reference locations, not observed shipping routes.
  • Solid lines have two reported directions. Dashed lines have one known direction; their amount is incomplete.
  • Line width uses a linear scale within the selected dataset and year. It resets across years.
  • Reported zero remains zero. Unreported trade remains missing. Inferred zeros are excluded from the historical reported-flow layer.
  • Historical source entities remain distinct. Modern reference names and anchors do not establish historical territorial equivalence.
  • Pairs without a verified map anchor remain available in the evidence browser.
  • Headlines are rounded for readability. Exact stored figures and source flags are available in the details.

Living-standard conversions

A proposed shared basic-needs unit requires a specified basket and local prices for the matching place and year. It has not been calculated across this trade dataset. Gold equivalents, household purchasing power and inflation-adjusted trade answer different questions and are not substituted for one another.
